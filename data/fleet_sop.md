# Fleet Standard Operating Procedures (sample)

> Illustrative, fictional maritime SOP content for a Bahri-style fleet operator.
> Not an official document of any real company.

## Cargo transfer operations

Before any cargo transfer begins, the officer on watch must confirm that the
ship-shore safety checklist is complete and signed by both the vessel and the
terminal. Cargo transfer must not start until the agreed transfer rate and the
emergency shutdown procedure have been confirmed with the terminal.

During crude oil loading, tank levels must be monitored continuously and the
maximum allowable loading rate for each tank must never be exceeded. The
responsible officer records ullage readings at least every thirty minutes.

## Chemical tanker operations

For chemical cargoes, the compatibility of the cargo with previous cargoes and
with tank coatings must be verified against the cargo compatibility matrix
before loading. Nitrogen padding is required for cargoes sensitive to oxygen or
moisture, and the inert gas system must maintain positive pressure throughout
the voyage.

## Ballast water management

Ballast water exchange must be carried out at least 200 nautical miles from the
nearest land and in water at least 200 metres deep, in accordance with the
vessel's ballast water management plan. All ballast operations are logged in the
ballast water record book.

## Bunkering

Before bunkering, the bunkering checklist must be completed and the scuppers
plugged. A continuous watch is maintained at the bunker manifold and at the
sounding pipe. Bunkering stops immediately if any leak or overflow is detected.

## Port arrival and departure

On arrival at a port, the vessel submits the pre-arrival notification at least
24 hours in advance, including the dangerous goods manifest where applicable.
The master ensures all statutory certificates are valid before entering port
state control jurisdiction.
