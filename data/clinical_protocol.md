## Hypertension management

For a new diagnosis of stage 1 hypertension, lifestyle modification should be
advised for all patients. If blood pressure remains above target after three
months, first-line pharmacotherapy is an ACE inhibitor or a calcium channel
blocker. Blood pressure should be reassessed within four weeks of starting or
changing therapy.

## Type 2 diabetes — initial therapy

Metformin is the preferred initial pharmacologic agent for type 2 diabetes
unless contraindicated. Renal function must be checked before starting and at
least annually thereafter. If HbA1c remains above target after three months, a
second agent may be added.

## Anticoagulation safety

Before starting an anticoagulant, assess bleeding risk and baseline renal and
hepatic function. Patients should be counseled on signs of bleeding and the
importance of adherence. Routine coagulation monitoring is required for warfarin
but not for most direct oral anticoagulants.

## Discharge checklist

Before discharge, confirm that medication reconciliation is complete, follow-up
appointments are scheduled, and the patient has understood their medication
instructions. A discharge summary must be sent to the primary care provider
within 48 hours.

## Sepsis screening

Any patient with suspected infection and two or more SIRS criteria should be
screened for sepsis. If sepsis is suspected, obtain blood cultures and start
broad-spectrum antibiotics within one hour, and measure serum lactate.
